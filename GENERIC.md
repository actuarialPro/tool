# GENERIC.md

Behavioral guidelines to maximize accuracy and efficiency when navigating, extracting, and synthesizing information from documentation, manuals, policy forms, and PDF reference materials.

**Tradeoff:** These guidelines bias toward exhaustive source verification and literal textual accuracy over speed or speculative interpretation.

## 1. Think Before Searching

**Don't assume the structure. Don't gloss over ambiguity. Surface terminology gaps.**

Before reading or searching a document:
- **Define explicit search parameters:** Identify core keywords, technical synonyms, and regulatory cross-references before opening the file. 
- **Acknowledge context gaps:** If a term, clause, or reference code in the document is unfamiliar or ambiguous, stop and clarify it immediately. Do not guess its meaning based on context clues.
- **Evaluate source authority:** Ensure you are referencing the correct version, amendment, or regional edition of the document before extracting instructions.

## 2. Literal Accuracy & Simplicity First

**Extract precisely what is written. Do not extrapolate beyond the text.**

- **No Speculative Interpretation:** Provide instructions based strictly on explicit text found within the document. Do not invent "implied" steps or add conditional workflows that are not documented.
- **No Over-Complication:** When summarizing complex documentation, preserve the exact procedural sequence. Do not create abstract workflows or separate guide files when a direct checklist from the source text suffices.
- **The Audit Test:** "Can a third party instantly verify this information by searching the exact keywords or page numbers provided?" If no, rewrite the summary to anchor it directly to the source location.

## 3. Surgical Referencing & Parity

**Touch only the relevant sections. Match the source structure perfectly.**

When extracting data or updating summaries from reference materials:
- **Zero Content Drift:** Do not rephrase specialized terminology, legal definitions, or technical naming conventions to make them "sound better." Match the source text exactly.
- **Isolate Your scope:** Extract only the information required to answer the specific query. Do not pull in adjacent clauses, historical footnotes, or unrelated guidelines unless they directly alter the rule.
- **Clean Up Orphans:** If a document update or new PDF version renders a previously cited section obsolete, remove that referenced material completely. Do not leave dead or conflicting references in the output.

## 4. Goal-Driven & Verifiable Extraction

**Define explicit page/section targets. Loop until facts are cross-referenced.**

Transform information-seeking tasks into verifiable verification checkpoints:
- **"Find policy rule"** $\rightarrow$ "Locate the exact subsection, note any active exclusions or riders, and verify the rule against the master index."
- **"Extract instruction steps"** $\rightarrow$ "Draft the operational checklist, then cross-reference it back to the original document text to ensure no regulatory dependencies were skipped."
- **Build Self-Reconciling References:** Every extracted fact, instruction, or data point must be accompanied by its explicit source location (e.g., `[Document Name, Section X.Y, Page Z]`). A summary is not complete because it sounds correct; it is complete when every claim traces back to an auditable page.
